How it works

Four steps. The last one is walking away.

  1. 1

    Add your images

    Drop them in, choose a folder, or pull frames out of a video. Each image gets a name you can @mention.

  2. 2

    Write the prompts

    Put ### between prompts and @mention the image each one uses. Or write one template and press One prompt per asset.

  3. 3

    Press Start

    For each prompt, FlowBatch attaches its images, types it into Flow or Gemini and submits it. It waits until the result has loaded, or until the site reports an error.

  4. 4

    Collect the results

    Each result downloads as soon as it's ready, named in queue order. Failed prompts can retry on their own, and you can pause, resume or stop at any point.

Start

From one video to a story

Turn one video into an animated story

Pull frames out of a video, turn each frame into an image, then connect the images with prompts that tell a story. Flow animates every scene from one image to the next, and FlowBatch saves the scenes in order.

  1. ride.mp4 · a frame every 0.5 s
    1Frame extractor

    Pull the frames

    Load a video file, a direct link or a playing tab, and pick an interval. Each step becomes a still named f_001, f_002 and so on, ready in your images.

  2. f_001 → anime image
    2Image mode

    Create the images

    Write one prompt with {asset}, like Convert {asset} into a 2D anime illustration, and press One prompt per asset. Every frame gets its own prompt, and every image is saved as it finishes.

  3. → → → →
    pic_001 … pic_008 · 4 scenes
    3Video mode · Sequential frames

    Connect them with a story

    Use these images for video brings the images back as pic_001, pic_002 and so on. One prompt per frame pair turns each pair into a scene, from a start image to an end image. Then write each scene as the next line of your story.

  4. 001 → 004 · in story order
    4Start · Frames to video in Flow

    Collect the scenes

    FlowBatch runs each scene through Flow's Frames to video, one after another, and saves the clips in story order. Play them back to back, or join them in any video editor.

The story, one scene per pair

Each prompt starts on one image and ends on the next. Because the frames come from one video, each scene picks up close to where the last one ended.

  1. Scene 1pic_001 → pic_002

    @pic_001 @pic_002 Night in the city. The rider pulls away from the curb, headlights sweeping across the wet street.

    001_pic_001_pic_002.mp4

  2. Scene 2pic_003 → pic_004

    @pic_003 @pic_004 The rider leans into the corner. The camera drops low beside the front wheel as neon streaks past.

    002_pic_003_pic_004.mp4

  3. Scene 3pic_005 → pic_006

    @pic_005 @pic_006 Onto the bridge. Wind pulls at the jacket, and the skyline opens up behind the rider.

    003_pic_005_pic_006.mp4

  4. Scene 4pic_007 → pic_008

    @pic_007 @pic_008 First light over the water. The rider slows down, turns and looks back at the city.

    004_pic_007_pic_008.mp4

Keep the look the same. Use the same style words in every scene, like 2D anime, cel-shaded, soft rim light, so the scenes match.

Place the images yourself. Put {start} and {end} in the first prompt, like From {start} the rider pulls away, ending on {end}, and each scene gets its own pair in those places.

An odd image out. With an odd number of images, the last one becomes a scene of its own using Ingredients to video. FlowBatch tells you before anything runs.

Beyond the queue

Everything around the prompts, too

Frame extractor

Turn any video into numbered stills

Pick a video and an interval, say every half second. FlowBatch saves a still at each step and adds it to your images as f_001, f_002 and so on, ready to @mention.

  • Use a file on your computer, a direct link to a video you host, or whatever is playing in a tab
  • Frames from files and links land at exact times, and Start and End take just a slice
  • Tab capture crops to the video itself, not the page around it

Split video

Cut clips and send them to Flow

Split a video into clips every N seconds, as MP4 that Flow accepts. Check each one in its player and untick the ones you don't want. Then save them, zip them, or attach the rest to Flow's prompt box in one go.

  • WebM or oversized clips are converted to MP4 before they're sent
  • Every clip reports back: In Flow ✓, or the reason Flow gave for refusing it
  • + Assets turns clips into references you can @mention in a prompt

Sequential frames

Animate your stills, pair by pair

Flow's Frames to video animates from a start frame to an end frame. FlowBatch pairs your images in order, pic_001 → pic_002, pic_003 → pic_004, and makes one scene per pair.

  • One click re-imports a finished image run, ready for video
  • With an odd number of images, the last one uses Ingredients to video instead, and you're told before anything runs
  • Queue rows are tagged FRM or ING so you can see the mode of every scene

Gemini

Prefer Gemini? Same queue.

Flip the switch at the top of the panel. Images, @mentions, the queue, downloads and ZIPs all work the same way. FlowBatch just drives Gemini instead of Flow.

  • Each prompt starts a new chat, so one result doesn't steer the next
  • Picks Create images or Create videos, and the Fast, Thinking or Pro model
  • Saves images at full size, and marks a prompt failed with Gemini's own words when a reply has no image

Publish queue

From results to a posting plan

Turn finished results into posts. Captions come from a template, hashtags are suggested from your own prompts, and each post gets a time. Then export one ZIP for Meta Business Suite or YouTube Studio.

  • Media, captions, a spreadsheet and a JSON schedule in one archive
  • Edit any caption or time, and untick what you won't post
  • It never posts for you and never asks for your logins

The small things

Built for long, unattended runs

Retries and pauses

Failed prompts retry on their own, or all together with one click. Pause lets the current prompt finish first, and Stop puts it back in the queue.

Tidy downloads

Results land in Downloads/FlowBatch/<project>/ as 001_f_001.png, 002_f_002.png and so on, in queue order.

ZIP only what you tick

Pack images, frames or results into one archive, untick anything you don't need, and choose where it's saved.

Credits before you spend

Shows your Flow plan and the credits you have left, estimates what a run will cost, and warns you before you start one you can't cover.

Keeps up with Google

Buttons are found by flexible matching. If Flow or Gemini moves one, Diagnose shows what was found and Pick fixes it in one click.

Remembers your work

Your queue, prompts, images and settings are still there when you close the panel and open it again.

Install

Up and running in a minute

FlowBatch isn't in the Chrome Web Store, so you add it to Chrome yourself. There are four steps, and you only do them once.

  1. Download and unzip

    You get a folder called Flow_batch-main. Keep it somewhere safe, because Chrome runs FlowBatch from that folder.

    Download FlowBatch (.zip)

  2. Open Chrome's extensions page

    Paste chrome://extensions into the address bar. Web pages can't link there, so you have to paste it.

    Then turn on Developer mode in the top-right corner.

  3. Load it

    Click Load unpacked and choose the Flow_batch-main folder, the one with manifest.json inside.

  4. Open the panel

    Pin FlowBatch from the puzzle-piece menu, then click its icon. The panel opens on the right. Open Google Flow or Gemini and sign in. If the tab was already open, reload it once.

You'll need

Google Chrome 116 or newer on a computer, and a Google account that can use Flow or Gemini.

Updating

Download the ZIP again and replace the folder's contents. On chrome://extensions, click the reload arrow on FlowBatch's card, then reload your Flow or Gemini tab.

Prefer git?

Clone the repository, load that folder instead, and update it with git pull.

git clone https://github.com/Manish-404/Flow_batch.git

Privacy

It runs in your browser, and nowhere else

No server, no account, no analytics

FlowBatch has no server of its own and collects nothing. Your prompts and images go only to the Flow or Gemini page you're using.

It only talks to what you point it at

That means the Flow or Gemini tab you're using, Google's file servers for your results, and a video link if you paste one.

Your account, your credits

It works in your own signed-in browser, just like clicking through Flow yourself. Google's credits, limits and terms apply as usual.

What it won't do

It won't download from YouTube, Instagram, Facebook or Threads, post to social media, or ask for any password.

Why Chrome shows these permissions
Read and change data on all websites
To fetch a video from a link you paste, which can be on any site. It also uses this to download your results from Google's file servers and to find the video on a tab you capture.
Downloads
To save each result into your Downloads folder.
Storage
To keep your queue, prompts, images and settings on your computer between sessions.
Tabs and scripting
To find your Flow or Gemini tab and connect FlowBatch to it.
Tab capture
For Capture tab, which records what's playing in a tab only when you press the button.
Side panel
To show FlowBatch beside the page you're working on.

This website has no cookies or analytics of its own. The Instagram reels on this page load from Instagram, which sets its own cookies, only if you press Load.

FAQ

Questions

Is FlowBatch in the Chrome Web Store?

Not at the moment. You install it from the ZIP on this page. It takes about a minute, and you only do it once. See Install.

Is it free?

Yes, FlowBatch is free. What you generate still uses your own Google Flow credits or Gemini limits. FlowBatch estimates how many Flow credits a run will use before you start.

Is it made by Google?

No. FlowBatch is an independent project and isn't affiliated with or endorsed by Google. It uses the Flow and Gemini pages in your own browser, the way you would by hand.

What happens when Google changes Flow or Gemini?

FlowBatch finds buttons by flexible matching instead of fixed IDs, so small changes usually don't break it. If a step fails, the Activity log shows which one. Open Settings → Flow elements (or Gemini elements) and press Diagnose, then use Pick to click the right element on the page. FlowBatch uses your choice from then on.

Where do my results go?

They go into Downloads/FlowBatch/<project name>/, named in queue order: 001_f_001.png, 002_f_002.png and so on. You can change the base folder in Settings, or pack any set into a ZIP and save it wherever you like.

Does it work in Edge, Brave or other browsers?

It's built for Google Chrome 116 or newer on a computer. Other Chromium browsers with a side panel may work, but they haven't been tested.

Can it download videos from YouTube or Instagram?

No, because those platforms' terms forbid it. Capture tab records what your own browser is playing, and From URL reads direct links to video files. Use them for footage you own or have the rights to.

Will it post to Instagram or YouTube for me?

No. The Publish queue prepares captions, hashtags and times and exports them. You then schedule the posts in Meta Business Suite or YouTube Studio. FlowBatch never asks for your social logins.
